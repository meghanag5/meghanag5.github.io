When the simpler model wins.

Predicting CRISPR off-target activity—and testing whether learned sequence representations generalize to unseen guides.

The problem

CRISPR-Cas9 can edit DNA at unintended sites. This project uses guide RNA and candidate DNA sequence pairs to investigate whether a model can distinguish sites with experimentally measured off-target activity.

The goal is comparative model evaluation, with an explanatory interface under development. These experimental scores are not clinically validated risk estimates.

What I built

  • Preprocessing and sequence checks for experimental guide–DNA pairs.
  • Mismatch features describing sequence differences, including their positions.
  • A logistic-regression baseline, a sequence CNN, and a combined sequence-plus-feature model.
  • Guide-separated evaluation, with decision thresholds selected using validation data.
  1. Prepare experimental pairsGuide sequence, candidate DNA, and measured editing labels.
  2. Represent the sequencePaired-sequence encoding and engineered mismatch features.
  3. Compare three approachesLogistic regression, sequence CNN, and combined model.
  4. Evaluate unseen guidesSeparate training, validation, and test guides; assess ranking and thresholded predictions.

Architecture summary of the project; not a live prediction endpoint.

Evaluation & results

The reported experiment used 1,087 training examples across five guides, 182 validation examples from one guide, and 353 test examples from two held-out guides. The test set contained 18 positives—approximately 5.1% of examples.

The key result

The combined model led on validation data, but logistic regression performed best on the held-out test guides. More complex models did not provide better generalization in this experiment.

At its validation-selected threshold of 0.544, logistic regression identified 6 of 18 positives, with 46 false positives, 12 false negatives, and 289 true negatives. Both neural approaches predicted no positives at their selected thresholds, despite producing ranking scores.

Why these decisions matter

Split by guide

Keeping guides separate makes the evaluation ask a harder, more useful question: can the model transfer beyond guides it has seen during training?

Inspect precision-recall behavior

With only 18 positives among 353 test examples, accuracy alone can hide poor detection. Average precision and the confusion matrix reveal failures that an overall accuracy number would miss.

Keep the baseline

The simpler model provides a necessary reference. The validation-to-test reversal makes generalization the central issue to investigate, rather than assuming deep learning is the improvement.

Limits & next experiments

  • Two held-out test guides are too few to establish broad generalization.
  • Only 18 test positives make the result sensitive to a small number of examples.
  • Next: evaluate across additional guide-separated folds and inspect guide-level errors.
  • Next: investigate the gap between ranking performance and thresholded detection.
  • Sequence highlights and a prediction interface remain in development.

Dataset context: Cameron et al., Nature Methods (2017), with HEK293 / sgRNA-transfection / three-day off-target measurements used in the project.
